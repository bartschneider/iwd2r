## Notes

Ported from the disassembly (0x7C8F70-0x7C997E, `ret 0x18` at 0x7C997E) and the Ghidra listing
above. The skeleton is the 32-bit sibling's (0x7CAC60, docs/evidence/7CAC60.md): allocate
`new BYTE[max(cx, cy)]` for the arc list, tint the ambient by `rgbGlobalTint` (+0xA4), derive the
three channel targets (`light + ((255 - ambient) >> 1)`, then `(v - 64) / cy * cy`), and paint the
two caps and the middle band from `GetEllipseArcPixelList` (vtable +0xC0) with the base scale 64
and `>> 6` (.rdata 0x85DE38, 0x85DE3A).

24-bpp specifics: three bytes per pixel, R at +0, G at +1, B at +2 (the little-endian byte order
of the 32-bit layout), and `lPitch` in **bytes** - `CVidMode::DrawLightingEffect` already passes
`ddsd.lPitch` for 24 (and `>> 1`/`>> 2` for 16/32). Each channel is `(pixel * scale) >> 6` and
clamped at 255 (`cmp ..., 0xff; jl` keeps, else 255 - 0x7C966D and the three copies); the byte
after the pixel is read in the original's DWORD load but never written, and ours writes only three
bytes.

The middle band differs from the 32-bit sibling's, and the port keeps it: the original's 24-bit
down ramp runs `nWidth - 1` times and peels one more pixel out of the loop (0x7C96F8 sets the bound
from `nWidth - 1`, no `>> 1`; the peeled pixel is 0x7C97E0), i.e. `nWidth` down steps after
`nWidth` up steps per row. `DrawLightingEffect32` ramps down `(nWidth - 1) >> 1` (0x7CB320: `mov
eax,[esp+0x50]; sar eax`) - a real difference between the two originals, not a port slip.

Quirks kept, with NOTEs in the source, as the 32-bit port documents them:

- the pixel list is allocated before the `cy == 0` test, so the original leaks it there (0x7C8F8E);
  ours allocates after the test;
- the original leaks it on the "all three channels zero" early return (0x7C90A9); ours frees it;
- the band divides by the row width (0x7C9612) and the original divides by zero once the arc has
  narrowed the row to nothing; ours stops drawing and frees the list.
- the caps' `pRow -= 3` and the band's `pRow += 3` are byte steps (the 32-bit code steps a DWORD).
