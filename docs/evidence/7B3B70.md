## Notes

The 16-bit counterpart of `BltBmp24To32Tint` (0x7CD870), and the tinted form of
`BltBmp24To16` (0x7B37A0). Three-byte BGR source rows, rows bottom-up (`lPitch` in
bytes, `pSurface -= lPitch / 2 + bmpSize.cx` per row), and the tint, add and
light multipliers from the image's `GetTint`/`GetAdd`/`GetLight` (all already
ported): the high-word flags reach them (0x10000/0x20000 tint, 0x40000 add,
0x200000 light), 0x80000 greys the source first `(r + g + b) >> 2`, the keyed
form (0x1) leaves pure green untouched, 0x4 turns a pure black source pixel
into half the destination's channel read back through its mask and shift
(`((mask & pixel) >> dstShift) << srcShift` + the brightness/contrast black),
and the light step clamps at 255 and moves green 255 to 254 so a lit pixel
never becomes the transparent colour. The pixel is packed through the mode's
source shifts (`field_C2`/`field_C6`/`field_CA`, `8 - the destination's bit
count`) and destination shifts (`m_dwRBitShift`/`G`/`B` at 0xAA/0xAE/0xB2).

Ghidra splits the unkeyed form off as `FUN_007b3ff8` (its `ebp`-relative
locals are this function's frame, i.e. a shared tail rather than a callable
function); the port keeps both forms in one body, as `BltBmp24To32Tint` does.

Widths below 2 do not take the single-pixel path `BltBmp24To16` has: the tinted
keyed loop is skipped for a zero width and runs per pixel otherwise, so a
zero-width blit writes nothing (the sibling's quirk does not carry over).

`RenderDirect` (0x7B3190) calls it for a 24-bit bitmap on a 16-bit surface when
a flag in the high word is set or the brightness/contrast move mid grey; iwd2r
runs at 32 bpp only, so the oracle case and the unit test are the checks.
